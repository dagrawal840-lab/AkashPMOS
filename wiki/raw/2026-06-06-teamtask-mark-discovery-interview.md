# Problem-Discovery Interview Transcript

**Product:** TeamTask (A fictitious collaborative project management tool)
**Interviewer:** Sarah (Product Manager)
**Interviewee:** Mark (Digital Marketing Manager)
**Duration:** 20 Minutes

---

Sarah: Hi Mark, thank you so much for taking 20 minutes to chat with me today. Just as a reminder, I'm recording this call solely for my notes and for our product team to review later. Is that okay with you?

Mark: Yeah, absolutely, that's fine.

Sarah: Awesome. To get started, could you just give me a quick overview of your role at your company and what a typical week looks like for you?

Mark: Sure. I'm the Digital Marketing Manager here at a mid-sized e-commerce apparel company. My weeks are pretty chaotic. I oversee a team of three content creators, two paid ad specialists, and I also work directly with our freelance graphic designer. My main goal is to drive web traffic and coordinate all of our seasonal product launches across our email, social, and web channels.

Sarah: Got it. That sounds like a lot of plates to spin. When it comes to planning and executing one of these product launches, what is your number one goal or what are you always working toward?

Mark: My main goal is keeping the chaos organized so we don't miss launch deadlines. A single campaign touches so many people—copywriting, photography, ad setups. If one piece is late, the whole launch gets pushed back, which directly affects our revenue.

Sarah: Tell me about how you manage that process today. What do you use to get those projects across the finish line?

Mark: Right now, it's a mix of things. We have a shared spreadsheet where we try to outline all the launch deliverables and dates. But people usually forget to update it. So, I end up sending a ton of emails, pinging people on Slack, and we have a weekly sync meeting just to make sure everyone is on the same page.

Sarah: You mentioned the shared spreadsheet and Slack. Can you tell me about the last time you ran into a problem coordinating a launch?

Mark: Oh, actually, just last week. We had a spring collection launching on a Tuesday. I assumed our freelance designer had the final web banners ready, but I hadn't checked the shared folder in a couple of days. Turns out she was waiting on the final copy from one of my writers, but they were never notified. I didn't find out until 5:00 PM on Monday. I ended up staying until 10:00 PM resizing and formatting images myself so we could launch on time.

Sarah: Wow, that sounds incredibly stressful. Why was that situation so difficult for you?

Mark: It's the lack of visibility. I'm spending half my week "managing the managers" or hunting down project statuses instead of actually doing marketing strategy. Because we don't have one central place of truth, I have to constantly bug people to see where things are. It burns me out, and it creates bottlenecks for the team.

Sarah: That makes total sense. What, if anything, have you tried doing to solve that problem?

Mark: We tried using a major project management tool last year—I won't name names—but it was way too complicated. It had so many features, folders, and customized views that the team refused to use it. If a tool takes more time to maintain than the actual work, it's not helpful for us. So we ended up going back to our spreadsheets and Slack.

Sarah: If you had a magic wand and could change anything about how your team communicates and tracks work today, what would it be?

Mark: I would want a dashboard that is visually intuitive. I want to log in and see exactly where every asset is, who is responsible for it, and if it's running behind, without having to dig through five different email threads. And it has to be simple enough that my freelance designer and my writers can just check off their tasks without needing an hour-long training session.

Sarah: That is incredibly helpful insight, Mark. We are about at the 20-minute mark, so I want to be respectful of your time. Is there anything else you think I should know about how you manage your day-to-day work?

Mark: I think you covered the main pain points. For me, it's just about saving time and reducing that last-minute scramble.

Sarah: Well, thank you again, Mark. I really appreciate you taking the time to share your experience with me. I'll be sure to pass these insights along to our design team. Have a great rest of your day!

Mark: You too, thanks Sarah.
