Mark is a Digital Marketing Manager at a mid-sized e-commerce apparel company who coordinates seasonal product launches across email, social, and web channels with a team of content creators, paid ad specialists, and a freelance designer. His core pain: no single source of truth forces him to spend half his week hunting down task statuses instead of doing marketing strategy.

---

## Participant

- **Name:** Mark
- **Role:** Digital Marketing Manager
- **Company type:** Mid-sized e-commerce apparel company
- **Team:** 3 content creators, 2 paid ad specialists, 1 freelance graphic designer

[Source: raw/2026-06-06-teamtask-mark-discovery-interview.md]

---

## Primary Job-to-Be-Done

[FACT] Mark's stated #1 goal is keeping campaign chaos organized so deadlines are not missed, because a single late deliverable cascades into a delayed launch and direct revenue impact. [Source: raw/2026-06-06-teamtask-mark-discovery-interview.md]

---

## Current Workflow

[FACT] Mark's current toolstack: shared spreadsheet for deliverables/dates + Slack for ad-hoc pings + email + weekly sync meeting. [Source: raw/2026-06-06-teamtask-mark-discovery-interview.md]

[FACT] The spreadsheet is chronically out-of-date because team members forget to update it, forcing Mark to chase status manually. [Source: raw/2026-06-06-teamtask-mark-discovery-interview.md]

---

## Critical Incident (Spring Collection Launch)

[FACT] The week before the interview: a freelance designer was blocked waiting on copy from a writer who was never notified of the dependency. Mark discovered the gap at 5:00 PM the day before launch and worked until 10:00 PM to manually reformat assets so the launch could go live on Tuesday. [Source: raw/2026-06-06-teamtask-mark-discovery-interview.md]

Root cause (Mark's framing): no automated dependency notification; status visibility depended entirely on manual checks of a shared folder.

---

## Pain Points

[FACT] **Lack of visibility** — Mark spends roughly half his week "hunting down project statuses" rather than doing marketing strategy. No central source of truth. [Source: raw/2026-06-06-teamtask-mark-discovery-interview.md]

[FACT] **Burnout and bottleneck creation** — constant status-chasing burns Mark out and creates downstream blockers for the team. [Source: raw/2026-06-06-teamtask-mark-discovery-interview.md]

[FACT] **Adoption failure with prior PM tool** — Mark's team tried a major project management tool (unnamed) and abandoned it due to complexity: too many features, folders, and custom views. Team refused to use it. Reverted to spreadsheets and Slack. [Source: raw/2026-06-06-teamtask-mark-discovery-interview.md]

---

## Desired Solution ("Magic Wand")

[FACT] Mark wants a **visually intuitive dashboard** showing: every asset, its owner, and whether it is running behind — all without digging through email threads. [Source: raw/2026-06-06-teamtask-mark-discovery-interview.md]

[FACT] Simplicity is a hard constraint: the tool must be usable by freelancers and writers with zero training overhead ("check off their tasks without needing an hour-long training session"). [Source: raw/2026-06-06-teamtask-mark-discovery-interview.md]

---

## Key Insights for TeamTask

1. **Visibility over features** — the primary ask is status transparency, not richer functionality.
2. **Dependency notifications** — the critical incident was caused by a missing automated handoff between writer → designer. Blocking-task alerts would directly address this.
3. **Adoption is the product** — prior tool failed on adoption, not capability. Simplicity and low maintenance cost are table-stakes, not nice-to-haves.
4. **Freelancer/external collaborator UX** — the freelance designer is a first-class user; onboarding friction must be near zero.
5. **Time-to-value** — Mark's pain is acute (late nights, burnout). He is not looking for a platform transformation; he wants relief fast.
